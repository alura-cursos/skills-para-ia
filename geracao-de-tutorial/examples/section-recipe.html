What you'll learn: the Stage 1 read-through cache that installs the big latency drop AND the peak-load resilience at once. What to cache, the TTL and key strategy, and the load-test signal that tells you it worked.

Stage 1 sits between the profiled baseline (uncached, p99 850 ms) and the DB-indexing stage that will polish the miss path. Its job is to install — in one layer — everything the service needs to survive load: a read-through cache, stampede protection, and negative caching.

The cache-the-right-layer principle: a single read-through cache at the object/query layer, with one consistent key strategy, beats caching fragments per-endpoint. Fragmented caches drift out of sync and leave the origin exposed on the paths you forgot. One coherent layer with single-flight defends every read path simultaneously.

The headline cost: p50 actually RISES slightly at Stage 1's end (120 ms → 135 ms). That's the price of the single-flight lock on the miss path; Stage 3 will reclaim it.

Worked example: the actual Stage 1 caching rollout

Setup. One cache.r6g.large node (13 GB). Read-through cache in the app tier, single-flight via an in-process request-coalescing map. Rolled out as a canary by traffic percentage behind a feature flag.

Execution. 0% → 25% → 60% → 100% over 40 minutes, load-testing at 5,000 req/s at each step. Watch hit rate climb and error-at-peak fall.

Infra cost. +1 cache node ≈ $95/month, plus ~3% app CPU for serialization. Cheap for the 4× p99 drop and 9× fewer peak errors.

Critical sanity checks. At 25% canary, p50 rose 6 ms (the single-flight lock) while error-at-peak dropped 11pp. We expected the p50 cost and tracked hit rate climbing. If hit rate hadn't passed 85% by full rollout, we would have stopped and checked key cardinality (too many unique keys → no reuse).

Takeaway. Stage 1 installs load resilience (the +19.5pp peak-error gain that §11 will trace through to Stage 4's 0.9% error) and costs a small slice of p50 that Stage 3 reclaims. The p50 bump is the goal, not a problem to fix — single-flight REQUIRES a request to sometimes wait for the in-flight refresh instead of stampeding the origin.

⚠ Bear trap: cache stampede at TTL expiry

Without single-flight, every 300 s the p99 spikes to ~900 ms for a few seconds — a latency sawtooth synced to the TTL. Cause: when a hot key expires, all concurrent requests miss simultaneously and hit the database together → DB CPU pegs at 100% → the request queue backs up → one slow query cascades into a wave of timeouts.

Fix: turn on single-flight (one request recomputes the value; the rest await its result) plus stale-while-revalidate (serve the stale value during the async refresh). Trade-off: once per TTL, a handful of requests pay the recompute latency. Acceptable.

You'll see this stage referenced in §[N+1] (Stage 3 DB indexing builds on the cache's miss path — a faster miss means a smaller stampede when one slips through) and again in §[N+2] (the ablation that proved skipping Stage 1 looks better in a light-load benchmark but is catastrophically worse at peak).
