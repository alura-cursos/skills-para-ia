N. Finding: Stage 1 caching is load-bearing under peak load

What you'll learn: a counterintuitive ablation result — skipping Stage 1 caching LOOKS better at the Stage 3 endpoint (+6pp SLO, −25 ms p99 in the CI benchmark) but is CATASTROPHIC at the Stage 4 endpoint under production peak (−56pp SLO, +16pp error). The mechanism: Stage 4's CDN can refine the load resilience Stage 1 installs but cannot bootstrap it from nothing. The methodological point: evaluating a stage at the intermediate endpoint can give the OPPOSITE answer to evaluating it at the shipped endpoint.

The "obvious" way to evaluate whether Stage 1 caching was necessary is to skip it and see how the rest of the pipeline performs. We did this. The result was so surprising it changed how we evaluate stage ablations.

The skip-caching variant — going from the profiled baseline directly into Stage 3 DB indexing — beat the real S1+S3 path by +6pp SLO compliance (and 25 ms lower p99) in the CI benchmark. With indexes in place and no single-flight lock, the uncached path is genuinely faster at low concurrency. The naive conclusion: caching was wasting a node.

But the actual ship runs Stage 4 (CDN + pool tuning) under production peak. When we continued BOTH paths through Stage 4 and re-measured at 5,000 req/s, the situation flipped: the skip-caching path lost its lead AND failed to hold up under load (a CDN offloads cacheable GETs but a cache-miss storm still stampedes the origin). The shipped S0+S3+S4 config lands 56pp below the real path on SLO compliance and 16pp worse on peak error — neither recoverable without adding an origin cache back.

How the experiment was run

Two configs, two load profiles, four test runs:

  1. Ablation A: applied Stage 3 DB indexing from the profiled baseline directly (skipping Stage 1 caching), with the EXACT same indexes and query rewrites as the real Stage 3. Saved as config ablationA-index-nocache.
  2. Eval at S3: ran the CI benchmark (500 req/s, moderate load) against both S1+S3 (real) and S0+S3 (ablation A), measuring p99, p50, SLO compliance, and error rate.
  3. Ablation A+: continued ablation A through Stage 4 (CDN + connection-pool tuning), with the EXACT same CDN and pool config as the real Stage 4. Saved as ablationAplus-cdn-nocache.
  4. Eval at S4: ran the production peak profile (5,000 req/s) against both S1+S3+S4 (real, shipped) and S0+S3+S4 (ablation A+) on the same metric battery.

Hardware identical across runs (same origin fleet, same 2M-object dataset, same traffic shape). Total experiment: ~3 hours of load testing ≈ $12 in load-generator instances.

Reading the table. At the S3 endpoint (CI benchmark, moderate load), A WINS on SLO compliance and p99 — with indexes in place and no single-flight lock, the uncached path is genuinely faster at low concurrency. Re-measured at the S4 endpoint under production peak, A+ LOSES catastrophically: SLO compliance craters to 40% and peak error hits 17%, because a CDN caches responses but a cache-miss storm still stampedes the unprotected origin. The only column A+ "wins" at S4 is p50 under light load (no lock overhead) — irrelevant given the peak collapse.

The arithmetic of the comparison. In the CI benchmark, A wins +6pp SLO and −25ms p99 → looks like caching wastes a node. Under production peak, A+ loses 56pp SLO and +480ms p99 → catastrophic. The shipping endpoint (production peak) matters far more than the intermediate one.

Takeaway. Stage 1 caching is load-bearing for the shipped system even though it looks wasteful in the CI benchmark. The mechanism is that Stage 4's CDN refines the resilience Stage 1 imprints (edge offload on top of a protected origin) but cannot bootstrap it from nothing — a CDN in front of an unprotected origin still lets cache-miss storms through (40% SLO at peak is not recoverable by adding edge caching alone). Continuing the no-cache path through Stage 4 spends its small p50 edge and never recovers peak resilience.

Methodological lesson worth publishing

For staged optimization where a later stage's payoff only appears under a load the intermediate benchmark doesn't apply, evaluating a stage at the intermediate endpoint (the CI benchmark) can give the OPPOSITE answer to evaluating it at the shipped endpoint (production peak). Re-run the full pipeline under production-representative load before concluding a stage is unnecessary.

Limitations of this finding

  • Scale. Validated at 5,000 req/s peak. Whether the same flip occurs at 50k+ is open — a much larger origin fleet might absorb miss storms without Stage 1's protection.
  • Cache design. Specific to a read-through cache with single-flight. A write-through or per-edge design might not show the same bootstrapping limitation.
  • One trial each. Both ablation A and A+ were single runs with one traffic shape; we didn't repeat with different burst patterns. The 56pp SLO gap at S4 is large enough that noise probably doesn't flip the sign, but we haven't proven it.
  • Stage 1 scope not separately ablated. Our cache covers all five read buckets. We don't know if caching only the top two (product + category ≈ 70% of reads) would preserve most of the resilience at lower memory. Worth a follow-up ablation B (not run).

Despite these limitations, the finding is strong enough that we ship S1+S3+S4 and recommend others do too — at least for read-heavy APIs under bursty peak load.
